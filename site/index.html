aispice reads your LTspice schematics, makes the change you ask for, and proves it in simulation on ngspice, LTspice, Xyce or Spectre. Every edit is a diff you can undo. On an open PDK it takes IC blocks down to a layout checked with DRC and LVS.

Edit

Changes you can review, not files rewritten behind your back.

The agent edits with typed operations: set a value, add a part, connect two pins. aispice places and routes them so the schematic stays readable in LTspice, outlines what changed, and keeps a snapshot for one-click undo.

  • Wires never short two nets
  • Ask before every edit, or apply and undo
  • Netlists identical to LTspice's own

Verify

Proof, not claims.

Write your requirements once as a spec table: corner frequency, gain, phase margin, ripple. After every change the agent simulates and checks each row, and tells you when one fails, with the margin.

  • Bandwidth, margins, overshoot, settling, THD and more
  • Waveforms with cursors in the app
  • aispice check fails a CI job when a spec does

Size

Values from an optimizer, robustness from Monte Carlo.

The model picks what to tune and the ranges; a numerical optimizer searches them with the simulator in the loop and snaps the answer to E24 values. A Monte Carlo run with real tolerances then says how many builds would pass.

  • Sweeps, worst-case corners and yield
  • Bias point of every transistor: region, gm, gm/Id
  • Poles and zeros, f0 and Q

IC design

Transistor-level blocks on an open PDK.

Register IHP SG13G2 once and a project simulates with the PDK's own device models on ngspice or Xyce. The agent sizes transistors by gm/Id from tables it builds, starts from templates proven at every corner, and checks each spec across process, temperature, supply and mismatch.

  • Six templates, from a current mirror to an LDO, at every corner of their set
  • The worst corner named for each spec; mismatch Monte Carlo on IHP's statistical models
  • Noise, PSRR and CMRR, input or output referred

aispice check two_stage.asc, at 15 corners on ngspice

Layout

From schematic to a checked layout.

Generators draw the block with IHP's own pcells: matched devices in common-centroid or interdigitated units, dummies and guard rings. IHP's KLayout decks check DRC and LVS, Magic extracts the parasitics, and every spec is reported before and after layout. When a check fails, the agent changes the generator's parameters and draws again; it never edits the GDS.

Chips

Blocks for chips, with ChipLeap.

A finished block becomes a macro package: GDS, LEF, liberty, a Verilog model, the SPICE netlist, specs measured at every corner and the DRC and LVS evidence. aispice and its digital counterpart, ChipLeap, check a package with the same rules. aispice assembles analog-on-top chips in IHP's pad ring with ChipLeap's hardened logic inside; ChipLeap places aispice's blocks in its digital chips.

Private by default

Runs on your machine.

No account, no sign-up, no telemetry. The only requests go to the model provider you choose, and with a local model through Ollama nothing leaves your computer. Keys stay in the operating system's keychain, and the agent's tools are confined to the folder you opened.

Do I need LTspice?

No. ngspice works out of the box. When LTspice is installed, aispice uses its parts library and can show each edit in LTspice.

Which models work?

Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, Ollama, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Frontier models do the best multi-step design work; small local models handle reading, editing and simulating.

Can it use my PDK?

Yes. Register an installed PDK with aispice pdk add (IHP SG13G2 from IHP-Open-PDK first): simulations load its device models on ngspice or Xyce, and layouts use its pcells and its DRC and LVS decks. A licensed PDK works through Spectre on your own server over SSH, and its model files never leave it.

Does it do layout?

For IC blocks on IHP SG13G2, yes: five generators (current mirror, differential pair, five-transistor OTA, two-stage op-amp, StrongARM comparator) draw with the PDK's pcells, then IHP's decks check DRC and LVS and Magic extracts the parasitics. Board layout is not part of aispice.

How does it work with ChipLeap?

ChipLeap is the digital counterpart: RTL to a chip with a pad ring. The two exchange hardened blocks as macro packages, checked by both with the same rules, so an aispice comparator can sit in a ChipLeap chip and ChipLeap's logic in an aispice chip.

Is it affiliated with Analog Devices?

No. aispice is an independent open-source project. LTspice is a trademark of Analog Devices, Inc.
